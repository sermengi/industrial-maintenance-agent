# Series 1710 Maintenance: Inspection Checklist

This representative maintenance excerpt summarizes inspection practices for a
horizontal centrifugal pump. It is intended as generic reference material for
similar pump maintenance workflows, not as a model-specific manual for CP-200 or
CP-300 assets.

## Safety, Isolation, and Mechanical Seal Inspection

Before inspecting the pump, place the equipment in a safe maintenance state.
Stop the driver, isolate electrical power, close suction and discharge valves
where the procedure requires it, and relieve pressure from the casing and seal
area. Allow hot surfaces to cool before touching bearing housings, seal glands,
or coupling guards. Keep the coupling guard installed during operation and
remove it only after lockout and verification that rotating parts cannot move.

Inspect the area around the mechanical seal, stuffing box, shaft sleeve, and
drain path for active leakage, dried residue, scoring, or signs that process
fluid has migrated along the shaft. A visible seal leak is abnormal and should
be treated as evidence for further inspection, but it does not by itself prove
the root cause of low discharge pressure.

When leakage is reported together with low flow or low discharge pressure,
check whether the leak is large enough to affect system performance. Confirm
that the seal chamber is correctly flushed or vented where applicable, and look
for secondary signs such as air entry, loss of prime, damaged elastomers,
loose gland hardware, or abnormal heat at the seal faces.

## Coupling, Alignment, and Vibration Checks

Inspect the flexible coupling for damaged elements, loose fasteners, missing
keys, and uneven wear. Check that the pump and driver remain properly aligned,
especially after maintenance, piping work, base movement, or a vibration event.
Misalignment can increase vibration, overload bearings, and shorten seal life.

If vibration has increased, compare the current condition with previous
alignment work and recent operating changes. Inspect the base, hold-down bolts,
guard clearance, and piping strain before concluding that an internal pump part
has failed.

## Routine Condition Checklist

Use the following checks during routine maintenance rounds:

| Area | Inspection item | Evidence to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seal area | Leakage, residue, heat, or damaged hardware | Leak location, severity, and operating state |
| Coupling | Element wear, loose bolts, guard contact, shaft alignment | Coupling condition and any measured offset |
| Bearings | Noise, heat, lubrication condition, housing vibration | Temperature trend and lubricant observations |
| Pump casing | External leaks, corrosion, loose fasteners | Location and severity |
| Piping | Strain, closed or throttled valves, blocked strainers | Valve positions and pressure readings |

Record abnormal findings with the asset identifier, date, operating condition,
and whether the pump was running, isolated, or already depressurized. Use those
observations together with operating data before recommending corrective work.
