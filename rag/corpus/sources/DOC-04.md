# Series HSCS Maintenance: Maintenance Schedule

This representative maintenance excerpt summarizes condition checks commonly
used for split-case centrifugal pumps. It is generic reference material for
maintenance reasoning and is not a literal manual for CP-200 or CP-300 assets.

## Bearing Temperature and Lubrication

Monitor bearing temperature as a trend, not only as a single reading. A bearing
housing that rises above the plant's adopted high-temperature limit should be
checked for lubricant level, lubricant condition, contamination, blocked
cooling or ventilation, incorrect grease quantity, and signs of bearing damage.

Too little lubricant can overheat bearings. Too much grease can also raise
temperature by churning, especially shortly after service. When a pump has
recurring bearing-temperature faults or repeated bearing replacements, do not
assume another bearing replacement is the only corrective action. Inspect the
lubrication practice, alignment, operating load, and installation conditions
for a broader root cause.

## Vibration and Alignment History

Record vibration readings during scheduled maintenance and compare them with
previous values. High or increasing vibration can indicate misalignment, base
looseness, piping strain, imbalance, cavitation, or developing bearing damage.
Alignment history is especially important when bearing temperature problems
repeat after bearing replacement.

## Scheduled Inspection Items

| Interval | Maintenance item | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Each round | Listen for unusual bearing or coupling noise | Early warning of rubbing, looseness, or bearing distress |
| Each round | Record bearing temperature and vibration | Establish a trend before failure |
| Lubrication interval | Check lubricant level and condition | Prevent overheating from lack of lubrication or contamination |
| After corrective work | Verify alignment and coupling condition | Avoid recurrence from installation or alignment error |
| Recurring fault review | Compare current fault with repair history | Escalate from part replacement to root-cause investigation |

For repeated overheating, combine the current temperature evidence with
maintenance history before recommending a consequential action. A lubrication
inspection and root-cause review may be more appropriate than another routine
bearing replacement.
