# Series 1710 Troubleshooting: Pump Runs but Delivers Too Little or No Liquid

This representative troubleshooting excerpt covers common hydraulic and
mechanical causes when a centrifugal pump is running but flow or discharge
pressure is below the expected operating range. It is generic reference
material, not a literal CP-200 or CP-300 asset manual.

## System Path: Suction Supply, Prime, and Discharge Restrictions

Low delivery can occur when the pump is not fully primed, the suction line is
not completely filled, or air enters the suction side. Inspect for a low source
level, air pockets, loose flange joints, leaking suction gaskets, blocked
suction strainers, or valves that are not fully open. Air entry can reduce flow
and make discharge pressure unstable even when the driver is running.

Confirm the discharge valve position and verify that downstream isolation
valves, check valves, filters, and strainers are not blocked or installed in a
way that restricts flow. A recently inspected discharge line with no blockage
reduces the likelihood of downstream obstruction, so the diagnosis should then
shift toward suction, seal leakage, impeller condition, rotation, or system
configuration.

## Rotation, Speed, and Impeller Condition

Verify correct rotation before extended operation after motor work. Wrong
rotation or low driver speed can produce low flow and low discharge pressure
without obvious external leakage. If rotation and speed are correct, inspect
for a worn, damaged, or obstructed impeller, excessive internal clearance, or
wear-ring damage that lets liquid recirculate inside the pump instead of moving
through the system.

## Seal Leakage, Internal Recirculation, and Checklist

External seal leakage can accompany low hydraulic performance, especially when
leakage is large enough to admit air or lose liquid from the pumpage path.
Mechanical seal wear, damaged sleeves, or loose gland hardware should be
investigated when low discharge pressure occurs with a current seal-leak
observation or a history of seal wear.

Use seal leakage as a strong inspection lead, not as a certain root cause. A
complete diagnosis should still compare suction conditions, discharge
restrictions, rotation, speed, impeller wear, and recent maintenance history.

| Symptom evidence | Likely checks |
| --- | --- |
| Low pressure and low flow | Prime, suction supply, discharge path, impeller condition |
| Low pressure after motor work | Rotation and speed |
| Low pressure with seal leakage | Seal condition, air entry, sleeve or gland damage |
| Low pressure with no downstream blockage | Suction, seal, internal recirculation, impeller wear |
